References and notes on general computing, command-line tools, LaTeX, and HPC.

- [The Missing Semester of Your CS Education, MIT](https://missing.csail.mit.edu/)

  This MIT course covers the tools that most computer science courses take for granted, such as the shell, Vim, Git, and debugging and profiling. Each lecture comes with notes and exercises.

- [Good Enough Practices in Scientific Computing, Greg Wilson et al. (PLOS Comput. Biol., 2017)](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1005510)

  Wilson and his coauthors describe practices that a researcher without formal training in software development can adopt, for data management, software, collaboration, project organisation, version control and manuscripts. The paper is a gentler sequel to their earlier Best Practices for Scientific Computing, listed next.

- [Best Practices for Scientific Computing, Greg Wilson et al. (PLOS Biol., 2014)](https://journals.plos.org/plosbiology/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pbio.1001745)

  In this earlier paper, Wilson and his coauthors set out eight practices for research software, among them writing programs for people rather than computers, automating repetitive work, making incremental changes under version control and planning for mistakes with tests. Good Enough Practices later reduced the list to what any researcher can adopt.

- [What Every Computer Scientist Should Know About Floating-Point Arithmetic, David Goldberg (ACM Comput. Surv., 1991)](https://docs.oracle.com/cd/E19957-01/816-2464/ncg_goldberg.html)

  Goldberg explains how rounding error arises in floating-point arithmetic and what the IEEE 754 standard guarantees about it. The link points to the edited reprint in Sun's Numerical Computation Guide, and the article first appeared in ACM Computing Surveys in 1991.

- [High Performance Scientific Computing, Randall J. LeVeque (University of Washington, 2013)](https://faculty.washington.edu/rjl/uwhpsc-coursera/coursera.html)

  LeVeque taught this course at the University of Washington and on Coursera in 2013. It covers the tools of scientific computing, including Unix, Git, Python, Fortran, OpenMP and MPI, and the [Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/academictorrents_cb91a3d7a4c4c086be240b54e83ed8d587b31ff5) keeps a copy of the course.
